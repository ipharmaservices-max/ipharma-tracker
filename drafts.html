Article

Belgian biopharmaceutical firm UCB marked a key milestone on Oct. 9, 2026, with the site dedication of its future biologics manufacturing campus in Gwinnett County, Georgia. The event moves forward an estimated $2 billion capital initiative that represents the company's first production facility on U.S. soil, strengthening its domestic supply capabilities for specialized biologic therapies.

A Milestone for Georgia Life Sciences

Situated on 79 acres within the Rowen innovation district, the planned facility is recognized as one of the largest life sciences manufacturing undertakings in Georgia's history. UCB leadership joined international and domestic officials for a ribbon-cutting ceremony, with attendees including Georgia Governor Brian Kemp, U.S. Department of Commerce Under Secretary Jeffrey Kessler, Belgian Consul General Katherine Raeymaekers, and Gwinnett County Board of Commissioners Chairwoman Nicole Love Hendrickson.

As part of the proceedings, partners unveiled Emmanuel Janssen Circle, an entrance roundabout stonework installation honoring the founder and family that launched UCB almost a century ago. The comprehensive project is projected to yield roughly $5 billion in economic impact, supporting more than 1,000 construction positions alongside an estimated 330 permanent, highly skilled jobs.

Digital-First Strategy and Local Footprint

Spanning approximately 460,000 square feet, the future site will employ modern manufacturing methodologies built on a digital-first philosophy. Operations are planned to integrate artificial intelligence and extensive automation to manufacture complex biologic medicines, establishing a resilient pipeline for both recently authorized therapies and forthcoming pipeline candidates.

UCB has operated its U.S. corporate headquarters in the greater Atlanta area since 1993. The company currently employs over 400 individuals in Georgia and more than 2,000 people across the nation. Chief Executive Officer Jean-Christophe Tellier pointed to the state's established life sciences network, robust infrastructure, and proximity to research and educational institutions as crucial factors in selecting the location. UCB intends to pursue collaborative initiatives with regional academic and workforce groups to develop the talent pool required for high-tech biomanufacturing.

Why this matters

UCB's substantial capital expenditure reflects a continuous trend across global pharmaceutical manufacturers seeking to onshore production of critical biologics within key commercial territories. By establishing comprehensive domestic production capacity, drugmakers insulate their supply chains from international logistics bottlenecks, align closely with federal manufacturing priorities, and enhance their ability to supply high-demand therapeutics directly to U.S. healthcare providers.

Additionally, the site's explicit reliance on automation and artificial intelligence showcases how modern bioprocessing infrastructure is moving away from legacy footprints. For industry leaders, facilities of this caliber set new operational benchmarks for biomanufacturing agility, yield consistency, and long-term compliance in a competitive market.

This article is based on an official press announcement distributed by UCB on Oct. 9, 2026.
